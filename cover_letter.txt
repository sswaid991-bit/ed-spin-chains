Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript "Practical Limits and Numerical Reliability 
in Exact Diagonalization of Quantum Spin Chains" for consideration 
as a Research article in Discover Physics.

Exact diagonalization (ED) is a standard baseline for quantum 
many-body methods, yet several practical choices inside ED codes 
are made by convention rather than measurement. We provide 
reproducible measurements, with stated uncertainties and 
limitations, for three of them:

(i)   the crossover between dense and sparse eigensolvers for 
      ground-state calculations, including measured Lanczos 
      iteration counts, and a systematic comparison between 
      numpy.linalg.eigvalsh and scipy.linalg.eigvalsh (a factor 
      of 2.3 difference at D ≥ 1024 on the same matrix);

(ii)  the correct finite-size ansatz for open chains, benchmarked 
      against the exactly solvable critical transverse-field 
      Ising chain, showing that a 1/N surface term must be 
      included to recover the bulk energy to 10^-4;

(iii) the benefit of symmetry reduction, quantified as a function 
      of system size for both the Heisenberg and TFI chains.

We also show that half-chain entanglement entropies of the open 
Heisenberg chain at N ≤ 16 give effective central charges 
differing by a factor of three between the two N mod 4 branches, 
a caution for small-chain central-charge estimates.

The manuscript is a benchmarking study. We do not claim a new 
method; our contribution is reproducible measurement with 
explicit limits. All timings are drawn from a single logged 
calibration session on the same machine, and all deterministic 
quantities (eigenvalues, iteration counts, sector dimensions) 
are reproducible on any hardware with the same library versions. 
All code, raw data, and figure scripts are openly available at 
https://github.com/sswaid991-bit/ed-spin-chains, including the 
exact session log with library versions and threadpool 
information.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, and all 
authors have approved its submission. The authors declare no 
competing interests. No human or animal subjects were involved.
